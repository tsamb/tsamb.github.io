## in the beginning

I like to claim that I was the first kid in New Zealand on the internet. My mum
got an email address in the late 80s so she could communicate with her techie
brother in Minnesota rather than writing him a snail mail letter once a month.
At the time, [dial-up wasn't available in New Zealand](http://downtothewire.co.nz/)
and so Mum had to wander down to the local polytechnic to email the only person
in the world she knew with email. As soon as dial-up was available at homes in
NZ, Mum signed up. (That must have been about 1992.)

So, as a kid, I was mucking around with Gopher, Finger and Telnet protocols
before the Web existed. I remember the first time I downloaded and played around
with NCSA's [Mosaic browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic_(web_browser))
on the family's Mac LCII. The Web was sparse back then and I preferred the fact
that I could download games from the Gopher servers I frequented. (Two days of
downloading [Realmz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realmz) on a 14.4k modem
introduced me to role playing games.)

I wasn't allowed consoles as a kid, much to my chagrin. I milked as much time as
I could at friends' houses who had Master Systems or Super Nintendos. Instead, I
got my early game fix playing classic Mac edutainment titles (shout out to
[Mario Teaches Typing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mario_educational_games)
and [Number Maze](http://www.myabandonware.com/game/numbermaze-3nx).) I graduated
onto the near-complete suite of Maxis titles of the 90s. SimCity 2000, SimTower
and SimFarm particularly held my attention.

More than anything, I liked to tutū around with the Mac. I hacked around with
settings, dabbled in HyperCard and was generally curious about what made the
computer tick.

## the middle ages

My only experience with PCs prior to my teenage years was at friends' houses
(playing games like Command and Conquer), at my dad's office (loading Total
Annihilation onto a handful of workstations and playing on the LAN) or at school
(DOS and Windows 3.1 machines with classic games like Commander Keen, Duke
Nukem, Cosmo and Ski Or Die). I lusted after a PC. And a console.

In my couple of years at high school, the computer lab machines were still
running Windows 3.1, even though Windows 95 had been out for years and XP was on
the horizon. On these machines I learnt BASIC. By this time, my family had
purchased a Windows PC for work Mum had to do. On it, I had already been playing
around with HTML, CSS and JavaScript as well as venturing into Flash and
ActionScript, back when it was cool. I even picked up a game design pack that
contained 3D modeling software and a C++ IDE.

I ended up cramming quite a bit into my four and a half years of high school. My
friend Richard and I won the second place prize in the national science and
technology fair. I placed in the top percentile in New Zealand in some
University of New South Wales computing competition exam. Instead of 5th form in
New Zealand, I spent 6 months with my Minnesotan family in junior year at an
American high school. I came back to New Zealand and completed my 7th form
classes in 6th form so I could go back and muck around in the States for another
semester. I received awards for top 6th form student in computing and English.
With my University Entrance certificate in hand, I headed back to high school in
the US to finish off the final semester of senior year at the same high school.

Upon my return to New Zealand, six months before I was to head off to
university, I worked on a building site as a labourer and studied part time
towards a teaching diploma to be able to teach speech and drama. I had been
involved speech and drama extracurricularly myself from the age of six onwards —
preparing for competitions for the first half of each year and for examinations
the second half. After working my way through all the Trinity College and New
Zealand Speech Board exams, I decided to get my own NZSB teaching diploma. This
ended up providing quite a good part-time self-employment opportunity through my
years of undergraduate study.
